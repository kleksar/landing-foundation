# Landing content

Read `AGENTS.md`, the current request, `PROJECT.md` and `SITE_SPEC.md`; inspect relevant supplied
materials and accepted voice constraints in `DESIGN.md`. If this is a new client or the brief needs
substantial clarification, follow `landing-intake` first. Work within the requested increment and
preserve agreed positioning, language, tone and permissions. For a narrow copy edit, change only
what is necessary; do not restart the page strategy.

The outcome is usable page copy and a content/behavior specification in `SITE_SPEC.md`, not a list
of suggestions that leaves the writing to the user. Keep the foundation's demonstration unchanged
when developing this skill; use a separate client copy for an actual brief.

## Ground the offer

Identify what the visitor is choosing or trying to do, which facts help that decision, and the
intended next action. A client-supplied claim is supplied information, not independent verification.
Keep a compact source note for material claims in the specification; no separate evidence database
or citation on every public sentence is required. Resolve substantive contradictions using the latest
accepted instruction, or the intake process if the answer is genuinely needed now.

Explain value through relevant supported details. Do not create business capabilities, operating
steps, time savings, guarantees, qualifications or results from what is typical in the niche. Retain
useful limits and conditions. Research may explain a topic or verify a requested fact; it cannot
establish what this particular client offers. Do not send private materials to public search.

Missing information belongs in working notes. Omit unsupported public claims and empty sections;
do not publish editorial reminders, invented examples of client work, or placeholders for missing
prices and reviews. Ask only if the gap prevents the next useful result, following intake rules.

## Shape the page around the decision

Choose and briefly justify the content order from the visitor's questions and available evidence.
A service comparison, an explanation, a sample or a compact offer can carry the page. No required
hero/benefits/process/testimonials/FAQ sequence, section count, word count or sales formula exists.
Use a section only if it adds a distinct answer; combine or remove repetitive material. Similar
patterns are acceptable when the task supports them; novelty alone is not a reason to add content.

Make the offer and relevant context clear near the start. Headings should convey useful meaning
when scanned on their own. Put necessary conditions beside the related offer or action. Do not
expand a thin brief into a long page, or remove details needed to make a decision just to be brief.

## Write the actual copy

Use the requested language and an appropriate voice for the audience. For Russian copy, prefer
natural sentences and concrete verbs over administrative phrasing and abstract praise. Preserve
necessary specialist terms and the client's character; clarity need not mean the same neutral tone
for an atelier, an engineer and an artist. Metaphor, warmth or humour may fit an agreed voice;
they must not obscure the offer or imply unsupported facts.

Write the visible headings, paragraphs, useful list/table entries and action labels. Avoid padding
such as a promise of an individual approach with no supporting detail. Read for meaning and rhythm:
remove interchangeable slogans, duplicated arguments, forced contrasts and keyword repetition.
Use persuasive specificity rather than manufactured urgency. Do not claim that text is human-authored,
will evade AI detection or will improve conversion without evidence.

## Define honest actions

For each material action, specify its visible label, actual effect, known destination and relevant
unavailable/error state. Opening an email draft, navigating to a section, requesting a callback and
booking a confirmed appointment are different actions. Use wording that describes the intended
implemented behavior; a service does not become available because its copy has been drafted.

If a file or form destination is missing, preserve the intended final action and record its release
prerequisite. Separate launch copy from any requested preview state; do not invent a download URL,
endpoint or success message. A temporary preview must not present a non-working control as working.
Use the existing intake rules for a useful interim action; do not silently replace the client's goal.

## Hand off and review

Keep `SITE_SPEC.md` readable and proportionate. Include the chosen content order and its purpose,
actual public copy, meaningful action behavior and observable acceptance for implementation.
Separate working notes, sources and missing inputs from text intended to render. Preserve applicable
routes, prerendering and accessibility requirements from the starter; replace demo-specific goals
and CTA acceptance when they are no longer the client's requirements. Mark proposed content as
proposed, implemented content as implemented only after checking code; drafting is not approval.

Before handing off, check the copy against the supplied facts; read just the headings and action
labels; check that each section adds information and that public text contains no working notes.
Correct concrete issues, then update `PROJECT.md` briefly with status, gaps and the next action.
Do not duplicate the whole copy there. Apply the root's scoped verification; for code behavior use
TDD. Content-only work does not require a build or browser. Stop at the requested scope.

For the editorial rationale and its limits, read [editorial basis](references/editorial-basis.md)
when assessing or changing these rules. Routine drafting does not require rereading external sources.
